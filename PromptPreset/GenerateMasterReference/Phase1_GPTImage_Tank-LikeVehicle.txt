Create a clean multi-view concept sheet of a stylized, deformed low-poly WWII-era tank.

VEHICLE:
[ VEHICLE NAME ]

The specified vehicle must remain clearly recognizable as that exact tank model or variant.

Preserve its characteristic:
- hull shape
- turret shape
- gun and mantlet design
- suspension type
- road-wheel arrangement
- track structure
- major hatches
- engine deck
- front and rear armor forms
- major external equipment

Do not accidentally convert it into another vehicle or another variant.

==================================================
STYLIZATION / PROPORTION
==================================================

Apply a deliberate compact deformation to the vehicle.

Most importantly:

COMPRESS THE VEHICLE IN THE FRONT-TO-BACK DIRECTION.

Shorten the overall hull length and wheelbase so the vehicle becomes noticeably shorter, stockier, and more compact than the real vehicle.

The deformation should read immediately as:

SHORT + CHUNKY + COMPACT

rather than simply as a small realistic tank.

Keep the vehicle width relatively substantial so the shortened length creates a strong squat silhouette.

The deformation should primarily affect longitudinal proportions.

Do not excessively shrink:
- turret size
- hull width
- track width
- gun caliber impression

The main gun may retain approximately its characteristic visual length so that it contrasts clearly with the shortened hull.

Preserve the original suspension TYPE and recognizable wheel arrangement unless a separate vehicle-specific instruction explicitly requests otherwise.

Do not arbitrarily remove road wheels, bogies, return rollers, or suspension units simply to shorten the vehicle.

Instead:
- reduce spacing where visually appropriate
- compress the longitudinal layout
- simplify geometry
- slightly exaggerate important components

while retaining the mechanical identity of the original vehicle.

Target impression:

REAL VEHICLE
→ compressed along longitudinal axis
→ simplified
→ chunky low-poly game asset

The result should have a stronger deformation than a realistic scale model, but should not become a toy or super-deformed caricature that loses vehicle identity.

==================================================
LOW-POLY DESIGN LANGUAGE
==================================================

Use a clean, deliberately simplified low-poly design.

Prioritize:
- large primary forms
- clear silhouette
- planar armor surfaces
- simplified mechanical components
- readable suspension
- clean turret geometry
- strong separation between hull, turret, gun, and running gear

Use controlled bevels only where needed for readability.

Simplify:
- bolts
- handles
- hinges
- track links
- grilles
- tools
- small fittings

Remove insignificant micro-detail.

Do not create dense realistic geometry.

The design should look practical to reproduce as an actual real-time 3D game asset.

==================================================
MATERIAL / TEXTURE
==================================================

Use an extremely simple grayscale material presentation.

MATERIAL:
- grayscale diffuse only
- subtle value variation if necessary
- very light ambient occlusion
- non-metallic
- high roughness
- nearly matte
- very low specular response

NO:
- realistic PBR surface treatment
- metallic reflections
- camouflage
- complex textures
- rust
- mud
- dirt layers
- scratches
- chipped paint
- heavy weathering
- procedural surface noise

The visual information should come almost entirely from geometry, grayscale diffuse values, soft shadows, and subtle AO.

Optional simple identification markings such as a plain white star may be used when appropriate, but keep them minimal.

==================================================
HERO ISOMETRIC VIEW
==================================================

The primary image on the sheet should be a large isometric / front 3/4 hero view.

Camera:
- slightly elevated
- approximately isometric
- minimal perspective distortion
- full vehicle visible
- clear visibility of turret, hull, tracks, and suspension

The shortened front-to-back proportion must be especially obvious in this hero view.

Do not use cinematic wide-angle distortion.

==================================================
REFERENCE VIEWS
==================================================

Include consistent reference views of the exact same design:

1. FRONT
2. SIDE
3. REAR
4. FRONT 3/4
5. REAR 3/4

The SIDE view is especially important.

It must clearly demonstrate:
- compressed vehicle length
- shortened wheelbase
- stocky proportions
- original suspension identity
- consistent deformation

All views must show exactly the same vehicle proportions and components.

Do not regenerate slightly different variants in each panel.

==================================================
DETAIL REFERENCES
==================================================

Include several useful detail panels appropriate to the specified vehicle.

Prioritize mechanically distinctive features such as:

- suspension / road-wheel assembly
- track and sprocket
- idler
- gun mantlet
- turret roof
- commander hatch
- engine deck
- rear hull
- turret machine gun
- distinctive armor structures

Choose only useful modeling references.

Do not overcrowd the sheet.

==================================================
REFERENCE SHEET PRESENTATION
==================================================

Present everything as a professional 3D modeling reference / game asset concept sheet.

Use:
- clean structured layout
- consistent model scale
- consistent lighting
- generous spacing
- neutral background
- subtle grid if useful
- minimal technical labels

Background:
neutral light gray, medium gray, or dark gray.

No battlefield scene.
No characters.
No dramatic environment.
No explosions.
No cinematic effects.

==================================================
LIGHTING
==================================================

Use simple natural / studio-like illumination.

- large soft key light
- weak fill
- subtle contact shadow
- subtle ambient occlusion
- neutral color temperature
- no dramatic rim light
- no colored lighting
- no glossy highlights

Lighting exists only to explain the geometry clearly.

==================================================
LABELS
==================================================

If labels are used, limit them to simple reference terminology:

ISOMETRIC
FRONT
SIDE
REAR
FRONT 3/4
REAR 3/4
DETAIL

Do not invent dimensions or technical specifications.

==================================================
PRIORITY ORDER
==================================================

1. Correct identity of the specified vehicle
2. Strong front-to-back length compression
3. Short, stocky, compact silhouette
4. Preservation of the original suspension type and mechanical identity
5. Clean low-poly geometry
6. Grayscale diffuse + subtle AO only
7. Large readable isometric hero view
8. Consistent multi-view reference
9. Useful modeling detail panels
10. Very low visual noise

FINAL VISUAL TARGET:

A recognizable historical tank transformed into a compact, shortened, chunky low-poly game vehicle.

It should look as if the original vehicle has been intentionally compressed along its longitudinal axis while keeping its defining mechanical features intact.


==================================================
DESIGN CONSISTENCY / MODELING READABILITY
==================================================
Establish one coherent stylized design and depict that same design
throughout the entire sheet.

Treat the large hero view as the primary design view.
Other views must explain that design, not reinterpret or redesign it.

Keep the following consistent across all views:
- primary proportions and silhouette
- number, order, and placement of repeated components
- component sizes and attachment points
- panel boundaries and major openings
- pose and orientation of movable parts

Repeated components may be hidden by occlusion, but must not be
added, removed, or rearranged between views.

Use orthographic or near-orthographic projection for FRONT, SIDE,
REAR, and TOP views. Avoid perspective distortion in these views.

Include a TOP view when it reveals important structure that is
unclear in the other views.

Detail panels must be close-ups of the same design.
Do not introduce additional parts or finer detail only in close-ups.

Preserve readable gaps, plausible attachments, and clear separation
between components. Keep circular parts circular unless their
deformation is an intentional feature of the design.

Prefer fewer, larger, consistent panels over many small,
contradictory panels.

Do not claim engineering accuracy, verified dimensions, or
game-ready topology based on the concept image alone.
